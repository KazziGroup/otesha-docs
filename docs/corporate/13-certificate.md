## Who can do this

Anybody on your organisation's account.

## Before you start

Set the **From** and **To** dates at the top of Reports. The certificate covers
exactly that period — *"a certificate is a period, signed"* — so setting the
dates is the decision, and issuing is the confirmation of it.

## Steps

**1. Open Reports and choose the Certificates tab.**

![The certificates tab in the corporate portal, with the issue button and the period column marked. Marked on the screenshot: Signs the period set at the top of the page. What it covers: a certificate is a period, signed.](/img/corporate-certificate-01-en.png "A certificate: its reference, the period it covers, and its totals.")

**2. Check the dates on the button.**

The button names the period it will sign — *Issue for 1 Jan 2026 – 18 Sept
2026*. If that is not the period you want, change the dates first.

**3. Issue it.**

## How you know it worked

A row appears with a reference, the period, its totals, and the status
**issued**.

## If it goes wrong

> **Note:** The **Document** column reads *Preparing…* at first. The PDF is
> produced after the certificate exists, so the record is immediate and the file
> follows.

> **Note:** The totals come from the same carbon series as
> [[link:corporate/esg-report|the ESG report]], and count only what was alive
> during the period. Where that series holds nothing for the dates you chose,
> they read zero — the certificate is still a valid signed record of those
> dates.

> **Note:** Issuing again for the same dates adds another certificate rather
> than replacing the first. They are records, not a current view, so nothing
> overwrites anything.
