Otesha is four applications, and these are their manuals. Each one is written
for the person who actually sits in front of that screen, so find the manual for
the app you use and ignore the rest.

- **Customer app** — for somebody sponsoring a tree of their own, on a phone.
- **Corporate portal** — for an organisation: bulk orders, invoices, the tree
  register and ESG reporting.
- **Admin portal** — for Otesha staff running the programme: caretakers, zones,
  species, prices, payouts.
- **Caretaker app** — for the person in the field, on a phone, often with no
  signal.

## How a page is laid out

Every task page answers the same six questions in the same order, so you can
skip to the one you need:

**Who can do this** — whether your account is allowed to. If it says staff only
and you are not staff, stop there.

**Before you start** — what has to be true first. Most of the mistakes these
manuals exist to prevent are really this section being skipped.

**Steps** — numbered, with a screenshot where a screenshot helps.

**How you know it worked** — what changes on screen. If that thing did not
change, it did not work, whatever the toast said.

**If it goes wrong** — the errors this screen actually produces, and what each
one means.

**Related tasks** — where to go next, and the pages that explain anything this
one assumed.

## How to read the screenshots

Callouts are numbered in the margin and listed underneath the image, never drawn
over the control they point at — so you can still see the button you are being
told about. Every figure also carries a written description of what it shows, so
the manual works if you are using a screen reader, and so an assistant reading
these pages can describe a screen it cannot see.

Screenshots are taken from the real applications, not drawn. If the screen in
front of you differs from the one here, trust your screen and tell us — the
figure is out of date, which is a thing we can fix.

## Conventions

**Statuses are printed in capitals** — `ISSUED`, `SUBMITTED`, `AT_RISK` —
because that is how they appear on screen and in exports. Where a status is
computed rather than stored, the page says so.

**Money is Tanzanian shillings** unless a screen says otherwise.

**Carbon figures are marked `±10% · indicative`.** They are estimated from
species and age, not measured. That marking is deliberate and appears wherever a
carbon number does, including in reports you send onward.

## What is not here

Some screens are deliberately undocumented, and the page that would have
covered them says why rather than going quiet. Usually it is one of three
things: the feature is deferred and its screen is no longer reachable; it needs
something this environment does not have, such as a map token; or it shows mock
data that would mislead anybody who followed it.

A manual that omits something without saying so is worse than one that admits
the gap, so where you find a gap, expect to find the reason with it.
